## 2. Critique

**2.1 The out-of-corpus result (C213, C214) cannot yet separate memorisation from scale.** C214 is scoped to one 330-file set (claims-status, C214 scope). The ledger already names a scale-compression confound (C213 limitation 1: out-of-corpus losses are 1.65-1.9 bits/char). A higher loss compresses relative gaps, so "the costs shrink out of corpus" is what the memorisation story and a loss-level story both predict. No queued item separates them: SQ410 bins by overlap, not by loss, and SQ194 tests formatting robustness on E0/E1. Also, I did not find a completed SQ213 calibration (coverage of the package-clustered bootstrap) in the rows I read. Until it exists, C214's interval has unknown coverage. (spec) 31 clusters is few for a cluster bootstrap.

**2.2 Self-speculative decoding is not yet a result for goal 4 or the lossless part of goal 6.**
- C19 ("identical output, just faster") shows only a blocker update as its latest evidence (claims-status, line 120). The v0.5.20 FAIL is not on that row. I did not open its five events, so it may be recorded there.
- The lossless criterion was revised after a smoke run, and the original criterion also fails (DEVLOG v0.5.20). The 30/30 pass with activation quantisation off shows a cause, not a fix. Exact losslessness needs batch-invariant matmuls, which are not built.
- The bench reports two rates: teacher-forced r1-vs-r2 agreement 0.869 (JSON line 165) and pooled acceptance 0.80 and 0.70 (lines 80, 93). Where I read, the JSON does not say which mode the pooled acceptance uses.
- (computed) With per-token acceptance a and draft length 4, expected tokens per verify round is (1 - a^5)/(1 - a). At a = 0.70 that is (1 - 0.168)/0.30 = 2.77; at a = 0.80 it is (1 - 0.328)/0.20 = 3.36. Both are before any draft cost.
- (spec) SQ403 (eval depth) shows r = 1 costs +27.9% on a checkpoint trained at r = 2 (quoted). Free-running drafts may drift from the verifier faster than teacher-forced agreement suggests.

**2.3 The A3 MoE core is justified by capacity, not by matched wall-clock, and "tie" has no margin.** C206's -3.3% is measured against a dense FFN with half the core FFN parameters (SQ401 text). A param-matched dense-2048 ties A3m at 20M (+0.15%, CI [-0.88, +1.20]), but the dense arm uses 45% more FLOPs (SQ401). On the E1 stack at 200M, C203 finds a param-matched dense FFN beats the MoE by 1.6% at about the same wall-clock. I found no ternary, 200M, wall-clock-matched test of the A3 core. The word "tie" also has no pre-set margin. (computed) The CI half-width is (1.20 + 0.88)/2 = 1.04 points, so the data rule out a gap larger than about 1.2 points, not a gap of 1 point.

**2.4 The byte-decoder cost is measured on the split whose inflation is the question.** C210's +14.4% is on in-training v2 val, not clean80 (claims-status, C210 row). K9 says 41.7% of val is near-duplicate. A decoder that spells memorised text can look cheap on a memorised split. So "not viable" rests on the contaminated split. C213's story predicts that the decoder cost shrinks out of corpus. Nobody has scored that.

**2.5 The data plan has an identification problem and a sourcing problem.**
- SQ291's scaling fit uses tokens proportional to parameters on 14M, 50M and 100M models (side-quests, line 312). Along that design, D = 20N, so N and D are collinear. The data can identify only the ratio, not separate exponents. A Chinchilla-form L(N, D) fit needs a design where they vary independently, and the M2 gate needs one.
- Tokens per parameter are not out of line with our runs: E1 200M / 11.1M = 18 (computed); the final plan 24B / 1.2B = 20 (final-model-plan, line 128). So the ratio is not the main risk. The open parts are the 8B codeparrot count (c), the 3-epoch repetition (r, SQ292 not run), and whether loops and unique parameters trade off the same way at 1.2B.
- SQ376 decides the data source. It is CPU-only, about 10 minutes, and has been "accepted" since the 12:40 review (side-quests, line 397). It is the cheapest decision on the path, and it has not run.

**2.6 Status semantics inflate the REFUTED count.** C5 is REFUTED because mean(E1) >= mean(E10) on a CI that spans 0 (claims-status, C5 row). A sign rule on a null reads like a loss to anyone who scans the table. Nine of 142 rows are REFUTED, and they mix real failures with nulls. A NO-DIFFERENCE status would keep the sign rule visible without the word "refuted".

**2.7 "Not needed" from two seeds.** SQ402 concludes the learned write strength "is not needed at 20M" from 2 seeds at -0.47% (beta fixed at 1) and -0.06% (beta 0.5), with no CI in the row (side-quests, line 423). This is the same error as 2.3: a null on two seeds is not evidence of equivalence.

## 3. Alternative ideas (speculation unless quoted)

**A. Loss-matched corruption control for C213 (CPU).**
- Hypothesis (spec): the out-of-corpus shrink of the three costs is a loss-level effect, not memorisation.
- Test: take about 1,000 in-corpus clean80 documents and rename a seeded fraction of identifiers to random names. Calibrate the fraction so E24's bpc on the corrupted set matches its bpc on the SQ99 set. Score A2s, A2f, E27, E24 and E20 (seeds 2-4). About 40 CPU-min (estimate, from SQ379's inference-only cost).
- Plan change: if the ternary and loop costs fall to their out-of-corpus values at matched loss, C213's mechanism is a scale artifact. If they stay within their in-corpus CIs, the memorisation reading stands and SQ410 moves up.
- Novelty: SQ410 bins by overlap, not loss. SQ194 covers reformatting on E0/E1. Closest: SQ410.

**B. Repetition and the loop-versus-unshared ordering.**
- Hypothesis (spec): under repetition, shared weights gain on unshared depth, because unique parameters have less new data to fit.
- Test: equal steps, 20M tokens seen in each arm: 20M unique for 1 epoch vs 5M unique for 4 epochs, for E24 (12 x 1) and A2f (6 x 2), 2 seeds. Eight runs at about 3.5 GPU-min each (B1 ran 23 runs in 80 GPU-min, computed), so about 28 GPU-min. The 20M x 1 runs may be reusable from B1.
- Plan change: if the ordering flips between 1x and 4x, the E61 shape choice needs a repetition arm. If not, the 4-epoch plan carries no loop argument.
- Novelty: SQ190 (repetition on E0/E1, not loops) and SQ292 (repeat penalty, E1 only). Closest: SQ292.

**C. Free-running acceptance for self-speculative decoding (about 5 GPU-min or 20 CPU-min).**
- Hypothesis (spec): free-running acceptance of an r = 1 draft against an r = 2 verifier is well below the teacher-forced 0.869.
- Test: on the A2s seed-2 checkpoint, greedy continuations of 64 clean80 prompts, 128 tokens each. Count accepted draft tokens with draft length 4, and compare the acceptance with teacher-forced agreement on the same prompts.
- Plan change: if free-running acceptance is under about 0.6, E8's premise fails at 13M and the 2.77 tokens per round above is optimistic. If it matches, E8 is unblocked on acceptance (speed still unmeasured).
- Novelty: SQ98 and SQ196 are teacher-forced. Closest: SQ196.

**D. Dense ternary FFN at matched total width, 200M, wall-clock matched (the A3 core control).**
- Hypothesis (spec): on the ternary A3mx stack, the MoE core's advantage over a dense ternary FFN of the same total width disappears at matched wall-clock.
- Test: A3mx with the MoE core replaced by a dense ternary SwiGLU of hidden 2048, seeds 5-7 (A3mx has those seeds). Three runs at about 11 GPU-min each (the 5090 rate in C1's note).
- Plan change: if the dense arm matches or beats A3m at equal wall-clock, the MoE core leaves E61's candidate list, as C203 suggests on the E1 stack. Otherwise MoE stays, with a wall-clock caveat.
- Novelty: SQ401 is at 20M; C203 is on the non-ternary E1 stack. Closest: SQ401.

**E. Recurrence-free core inside the assembled recipe.**
- Hypothesis (spec): inside A2s, replacing each GDN with a depthwise long convolution at matched parameters costs little. Then the linear-time pillar is not what the assembly gain rests on.
- Test: A2s vs A2s with a conv in place of each GDN, 20M, 2 seeds, about 15 GPU-min.
- Plan change: a loss within seed noise of A2s weakens the fusion story for the recipe. A clear loss supports GDN as a component.
- Novelty: SQ215 runs this control on E1, not on the assembled recipe. Closest: SQ215.

**F. Weight averaging of the ternary latent weights.**
- Hypothesis (spec): part of the ternary cost (+9.5%, C207) is end-of-schedule code flipping, which averaging the latent weights reduces.
- Test: A2s vs A2s with an EMA (0.999) on latent weights, scoring the EMA weights, 20M, 2 seeds, about 15 GPU-min.
- Plan change: recovering 2 points of the 9.5 (computed: 2/9.5 = 21%) would mean part of the ternary cost is optimiser noise, and E61 needs an EMA arm. A1f's null (start fraction, +0.1%) lowers the prior.
- Novelty: a grep for EMA and averaging in side-quests returned nothing. Closest: SQ430 (decay) and A1f (late ternary start).

## 4. The one experiment I would run next

Run A, the loss-matched corruption control, first. It uses existing checkpoints, needs no GPU, and tests the mechanism behind the out-of-corpus result that the README says the final model will be judged on.

- **Prediction (spec, register before running):** the costs on the loss-matched corrupted set are either near their in-corpus values (memorisation) or near their out-of-corpus values (scale). I expect the second, but the prediction is a guess.
- **Decision rule (spec):** if the ternary cost on the corrupted set is within 1 point of its out-of-corpus value (+0.2%) and the loop cost reverses sign, C213's mechanism is downgraded to a scale artifact. If both costs stay within their in-corpus CIs, the memorisation reading stands and SQ410 moves to the front. Anything between is reported as mixed.
- **Caveat (spec):** renaming reproduces the loss level but probably not the error types of library code, so a "scale" result would be a weaker finding than it looks.
