### 2026-11-22 20:20 · v0.4.71 · Registered
**Question:** do results transfer from the N1X (GB10-family, sm_121) to a rented RTX 3090 (sm_86), and how much faster is the 3090? This decides whether to scale to 8x (`notes/compute-rental.md`).

**Design:** same code at commit `9712842` (v0.4.70, a clean `git clone` of the pushed commit, run with `--require-clean`), the same dataset (sha256-verified upload), and the same software (torch 2.14.1+cu130, triton 3.8.0, fla 0.6.0, Python 3.12). Only the GPU differs.
- Runs: K7's bridge pair at seed 0, lr 3e-3, 20M tokens, `--compile`:
  - E10 (`configs/e10_llama_ref.json`), N1X reference 1.0286 val_loss, 2.0 min wall;
  - E1 (`configs/e1_hybrid.json`), N1X reference 1.0071, 2.7 min wall.
- Then 60-step `--bench` runs of E0, E1 and E2 for step time and peak memory.

**Predictions (registered before any 3090 number is seen):**
1. **P1, loss transfer:** for both E10 and E1, |val_loss(3090) − val_loss(N1X)| ≤ 0.004. That is the provisional `val_loss_abs_floor` in `notes/reproducibility.md`; this is a cross-hardware reproduction, so not bitwise.
   - Expected verdict: `STATISTICAL`.
   - If P1 fails on either model, cross-GPU results are **not** pooled with N1X results, and every comparison stays on one SKU (already the rule in `notes/compute-rental.md`).
2. **P2, ordering:** E1 < E10 at this LR, as on the N1X (gap 0.0215). This is not a confirmatory test (n = 1 seed, and K7 is tuning, not testing).
3. **P3, throughput (informational):** the 3090's train tokens/s is 2–3× the N1X's, based on bandwidth (~936 vs ~300 GB/s; training is bandwidth-bound per `notes/hardware-spark.md`). A ratio below 1.5× means renting buys parallelism only, not speed.
4. **P4, memory:** peak memory ≤ 12 GB for E0, E1 and E10 at B = 32 × T = 1024 (the N1X measured 8.9–10.4 GB). E2 fits in 24 GB.

**Cost guard:** on-demand at $0.232/hr. The approved cap is $30 total / 12 h. Destroy the instance as soon as the jobs finish. (Source: `vastai show instances`; the human's approval in chat, 2026-11-22.)

### 2026-11-22 20:35 · v0.4.71 · Result (3090 part)
- **Config:** commit `9712842`, clean checkout, `--require-clean`. Exact fields (steps 611, tokens 20,021,248, FLOPs, params) match the N1X runs, so the recipe is identical.
- **Losses:**

| | N1X val_loss | 3090 val_loss | Δ | N1X tok/s (median, step ≥ 100) | 3090 tok/s | ratio | peak mem N1X / 3090 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E10 | 1.0286 | **1.0306** | +0.0020 | 202.0k | 272.5k | **1.35×** | 4.39 / 4.34 GB |
| E1 | 1.0071 | **1.0078** | +0.0007 | 138.0k | 254.5k | **1.84×** | 7.10 / 7.06 GB |

- **P1 passes** (both |Δ| ≤ 0.004; verdict `STATISTICAL`).
- **P2 holds:** E1 < E10, with a gap of 0.0228 vs 0.0215 on the N1X.
- **P3 is a partial miss:** steady-state 1.35× / 1.84×, against the predicted 2–3×. The bandwidth model over-predicts at 13.8M params, so the runs are probably overhead-bound.
- **Whole-run tokens/s is misleading on a fresh box.** E1 ran 124.8k vs the N1X's 132.4k (0.94×) because of the cold Triton/fla autotune cache, about 1 min of fixed cost per new box. Report steady-state and the cold-start cost separately.
- **P4 holds:** peak memory 4.3–7.1 GB, and E2 peaks at 12.0 GB (from a 60-step bench).
- **Provisioning cost:**
  - env build about 5 min (first time), and an idempotent re-run 19 s;
  - dataset upload 494 s at about 1 MB/s over the operator's link, the dominant setup cost.
- **Fix for provisioning:** `cockpit/remote/fetch_data.sh` rebuilds the dataset on the box from Hugging Face and verifies it against `experiments/data_integrity/dataset.manifest.json`.

### 2026-11-22 21:10 · v0.4.73 · Result (5090 part, plus the three-platform analysis)
- **Loss:**
  - E10: **1.0297** (Δ +0.0011 vs the N1X);
  - E1: **1.0051** (Δ −0.0020).

  5-P1 passes. E1 < E10 holds on all three machines.
- **Steady-state median tokens/s:**
  - E10: 845k, 3.10× the 3090 and 4.18× the N1X;
  - E1: 743k, 2.92× the 3090 and 5.38× the N1X.

  5-P2 (≥ 1.10× / 1.25× the 3090) passes by a wide margin. The "overhead-bound" reading from the 3090 part was wrong, or at least specific to that host: the 5090 shows the GPU does matter.
- **Cold-compile cost on a fresh box:** about 29 s for E1 (0.954 vs 0.471 min warm).
- **Value (E1 steady-state; N1X electricity only, assumed):**

| | N1X | RTX 3090 | RTX 5090 |
|---|---|---|---|
| $/hr | ~0.02 (assumed) | 0.232 | 0.523 |
| tok/s E1 | 138k | 254.5k | **743k** |
| B tokens / hr | 0.50 | 0.92 | **2.67** |
| $ per 1B tokens | ~0.04 | 0.253 | **0.196** |
| 200M-token E1 run | 24.2 min | 13.1 min | **4.5 min** |
| $ per 200M-token run | ~0.01 | 0.051 | **0.039** |

- **5-P3** (within ±30% of the 3090's $/token): passes, at 23% cheaper for E1 and 27% for E10.
- **Per-box fixed overhead:** about 3.3 min (76 s env, about 90 s data, about 30 s compile), about $0.03 on a 5090. It amortises after one or two 200M-token runs.
- **Decision:** the 5090 is the default rental SKU at this model size; it wins both value per dollar and time to result. Cheaper cards (3060, A4000, 5060 Ti) are still unmeasured; a 10-min benchmark sweep would settle them (~$0.10).
