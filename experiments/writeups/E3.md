## What it will test, in plain English

> **Can the model still learn if every one of its numbers can only be −1, 0 or +1?**
>
> Normally each of a model's millions of parameters is stored as a precise 16-bit number. "Ternary" weights (the BitNet b1.58 idea) allow just three values. That makes the model roughly **10× smaller to store**, and much of the maths turns into simple additions, which matters a lot for small devices and for chips like FPGAs.
>
> The question is the price: how much worse does the model get? And does the rounding error pile up when the same layers are reused in a loop?

## Why it matters

Ternary weights are the third FusionDance ingredient: they cut the **bits per parameter**, where looping cuts the **number** of parameters and the linear-time layers cut the **cost of long text**.
