## What it will test, in plain English

> **A one-setting change to our "running notes" layer turned out to be amazing at keeping track of things. Does it also make the model better at writing code, or at least no worse?**
>
> Our Gated DeltaNet layers keep a small notebook of what they've read. Normally, each new word can only **partly overwrite** an old note. With "negative eigenvalues", a new word can also **flip** a note: write its opposite. That sounds odd, but it's exactly what you need for tasks like "is the number of 1s so far odd or even?", where each new 1 flips the answer.
>
> In a small test (side quest SQ6), this one change scored **100%** on that kind of task and kept working on sequences **twice as long** as it trained on. Our loop trick got 98%, but fell to 61% on the longer sequences.
>
> E11 checks whether this change also helps, or at least doesn't hurt, the real job of predicting Python code. It's our best model (E1) with only this setting changed.

## Why it matters

FusionDance's original bet was that looping would give the model the "keep track of state" ability that these fast layers lack. If a free one-setting change does that better, the loop may be needed for something else, or not at all. That would be a big, useful surprise.
