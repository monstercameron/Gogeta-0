## 2. Critique of the model and the method

**2.1 A1a and A1i cost about 17% more compute per step (code-read + computed, new).** model.py:347-350 runs the first r - bptt_loops passes under no_grad. Counting forward passes as units F and backward as 2F, a step with r loops costs rF + 2 min(r, 4)F. Under r ~ U{1..7} (E27) the mean is 72/7 = 10.3F. Fixed r = 4 (A1a) costs 12F (+17%). bptt_loops = 7 (A1i) costs 3r, mean 12F (+17%). The FLOP table (`flops_per_token`) multiplies every pass by 3, so it shows none of this; only tokens per second does. Two consequences. First, an A1a or A1i "win" at matched tokens partly buys compute, so each effect must be reported beside step compute, and A1a and A1i need an E27 control at matched compute. Second, F2's FLOP match overstates loop-cell training cost by about 17% (modelled 12F vs actual 10.3F), so the loop penalties in F2 are, if anything, understated per actual compute. Both points need stating in C202 and C201.

**2.2 The noise estimate that sets the -1.0% threshold is too thin (computed).** The three E27 seed values quoted in PREDICTIONS give a sample sd of 0.0018 (0.55%) on 2 degrees of freedom. The 95% chi-square interval for that sd is roughly 0.3% to 1.1%. The "about 2 seed sd" wording and the 20-25% false-winner estimate both rest on this one number. Estimate the noise from the placebo arm A1z run on the same seeds, and from the 200M full-fusion replicate runs (SQ263 cell 111, 3 seeds, if same config). Report the false-winner rate by simulation over the arms, not from the formula.

**2.3 A2 is underpowered as drafted (spec + computed).** Three fresh seeds give 2 df per paired contrast. A per-seed paired-difference sd near 0.8% (sqrt 2 x 0.55%) puts the SE of a 3-seed mean near 0.45%, so a 1% effect is only about 2.2 SE. Use 5 fresh seeds (2-6) for each primary contrast and a pre-registered minimum effect size (1%) for any winner. Cost (computed): 8 arms x 5 seeds x about 9 min per 200M run of the fused cell (F2 median 0.92 min per 20M run, x10) is about 6 box-hours, about $3.3 at the A1 rate. That fits the $25 cap.

**2.4 I is one contrast, and its LR coverage is incomplete.** The cells I can find in the sources are 000, 100, 010, 001 and 111 (SQ263). I is a single 2x2x2 contrast over those five. I found no 110, 101 or 011 cell in the sources I read, so the pairwise terms (GDN x loops, GDN x ternary, loops x ternary) are not identified there. "The ingredients compound" therefore rests on one contrast plus the SQ337 trajectory. The LR gap (SQ342) has no fixed sign: an inflated y000 pushes I up, an inflated y100 pushes it down. Its effect on C202 is unknown in either direction, so SQ342 should run before any A2 comparison that uses cell 000 or 100 as a reference.

**2.5 The efficiency pillar is not tested where it applies (quoted).** Every quality number is at T = 1,024 (`max_seq_len`, model.py), where quadratic costs are small. The linear-time benefit is analytic for the full stack (C12: our SWA is a dense-masked kernel, C15 only at kernel level). SQ338's wall-clock depends on the fla GDN kernel and the dense-masked SWA, so it will move when SQ72 (flex SWA) and SQ77 (fused GDN) land. A2 should report tok/s next to each cell and at least one length of 4k or more (SQ237 and SQ351 are the only relevant items; both are GPU-only or CPU-only and not yet run). Without that, "competitive" means "competitive per FLOP at 1k".

**2.6 The bar for "competitive" is not written down.** A1 asks whether arms recover part of E27's gap to dense. The question the final-model plan needs is whether the assembly beats an unshared stack at matched active FLOPs. Pre-register three A2 contrasts: (a) assembled vs E27 (stack effect); (b) assembled vs dense E20 at matched active FLOPs (fusion gap); (c) assembled vs an unshared 14-block stack at matched active FLOPs (the C197 reference, with about 2.3x the unique parameters; report both FLOP-matched and param-matched). Proposed rule (spec): "competitive" means within 1% of (c) at matched FLOPs, or better at equal wall-clock. If the rule fails, record that the loop and ternary fusion is not competitive, and plan E61 around the unshared lever.

**2.7 Attribution in A2.** Stack the A1 winners, then remove each one (leave-one-out, k+1 arms), not add them pairwise. This keeps the one-change rule at the stack level and shows which winners are redundant. Every arm uses the same fresh seeds, paired.

**2.8 Dataset labels.** C197 and C203 are on code-py-v1; A1 and F2 are on v2. A2's reference arms (unshared, dense) must be re-run on v2, or the cross-reference must be labelled as cross-dataset in the ledger row (SQ238 shows the dataset change alone moves E1 by about 2%).

## 3. Alternative ideas (6)

Each idea: hypothesis (spec), cheapest test, the result that would change plans, and novelty against side-quests.md and the queue.

**I-A. Elastic unshared depth instead of loops.** Hypothesis (spec): per-block layer drop (p = 0.1) on an unshared 14-block stack gives a variable-compute knob without weight sharing, keeping most of C197's quality at 10 blocks. Test: E32 config at 20M, 2 seeds, drop 0 vs 0.1, evaluated at 14, 12 and 10 blocks; about 4 runs x 1.5 GPU-min (computed from the F2 20M medians, 0.58-0.94 min). Changes plans: if the dropped model at 10 blocks is within about 1% of its own 14-block loss and the no-drop model at 10 blocks is more than 2% worse, loops are not needed for variable compute, so E61 and A2 drop loops. Novelty: SQ20 and SQ34 vary the loop count; no layer-drop arm appears in side-quests or the queue.

**I-B. Warm-started tying (loop-ification).** Hypothesis (spec): part of the loop penalty is optimisation, because tied weights start from an init that suits no single pass. Start from an unshared 20M checkpoint, tie passes at 50% of tokens, and continue; compare with a fresh loop run at equal total compute. Test: 2 runs at 20M, about 3 GPU-min each, if the unshared checkpoint was kept (check whether SQ282's E32 20M checkpoints exist). Changes plans: if warm-start tying recovers at least half the 20M loop penalty, loops are optimisation-limited and A2 uses a warm-start curriculum; if it recovers little, capacity explains the penalty, consistent with C197. Novelty: SQ31, SQ36 and SQ16 target norm and init, not a warm-start schedule.

**I-C. A decode-time frontier.** Hypothesis (spec): at batch 1 a looped model costs about r times the block compute per token, so quality per decode-second may reverse the loop verdict that quality per training FLOP gives. Test: GDN-only stacks at r = 1, 2, 4 and the unshared equivalent, measuring per-token latency and state bytes with the verified fused-recurrent decode path (C111, 0.036 ms per step). Attention blocks have no KV cache yet (gogeta/hf.py), so restrict to GDN-only stacks. About 5 GPU-min. Changes plans: if r = 4 costs 3x or more per decode step for under 1% quality gain, drop loops from E61 whatever the training results say. Novelty: SQ53, E8 and C19 build decode but measure no quality-latency frontier; SQ338 is training wall-clock only.

**I-D. Share the MLP, keep per-pass mixers (the reverse split of SQ32).** Hypothesis (spec): the loop penalty lives mostly in the time-mixing state, since the position-wise MLP tolerates sharing. The MLP holds about 60% of a GDN block's weights at d = 384 (computed: 1.18M vs 0.74M for q, k, v, gate, o). Test: TINY (d = 128) at 20M, 2 seeds, shared-MLP with unique mixers vs shared-mixer with unique MLPs (SQ32's arm) vs full loop, parameter-matched by construction; about 10 GPU-min or CPU if fast enough. Changes plans: if unique mixers carry the gain, the fused cell keeps per-pass mixer state and shares MLPs, moving the unique-parameter budget to mixers. Novelty: SQ32 covers the other split only.

**I-E. GDN state capacity at matched parameters.** Hypothesis (spec): the fused cell's penalty is partly state overwrite, because each pass rewrites a per-head state of H x hd^2 (6 x 64^2 = 24,576 per layer, computed). Test: head configs 3x128, 6x64, 12x32 at matched parameters, cells 100 and 111, 20M, 2 seeds: 6 runs, about 12 GPU-min. Changes plans: if I moves by more than 1 point across head configs, the anti-synergy is a state-capacity effect and A1 needs a head-count arm. Novelty: SQ330 covers decay horizon; no side-quest varies state size at matched parameters.

**I-F. Ternary final-iterate sensitivity (CPU, no training).** Hypothesis (spec): part of the ternary penalty comes from final weights that sit near absmean rounding thresholds, so the loss moves under small weight noise. Test: on the existing 200M checkpoints, cells 001 and 111 vs 000 and 100, measure clean80 loss under weight jitter of 5% of the layer scale and the fraction of ternary weights within that margin of a rounding threshold; about 5 CPU-min. Changes plans: if jitter moves the ternary cells by more than the whole cell gap, the 200M I is partly a checkpoint-noise effect, and the registered metric should average the last checkpoints. Novelty: SQ339 splits the quantisation post hoc and SQ354 measures curvature; neither measures threshold sensitivity.

## 4. The one experiment I would run next

**I-A (elastic unshared depth), registered before running.** Why: it is the cheapest test of the question every A1 loop arm presupposes, namely whether looping is the right way to get variable compute. It needs one layer-drop flag and no new architecture, costs about 6 GPU-min (computed), and its answer decides which base A2 stacks on. Prediction (spec): the drop-trained model at 10 blocks is within 1.0% of its own 14-block loss, and the no-drop 14-block model at 10 blocks is more than 2% worse. Falsifier: layer drop costs more than 1% at full depth, so elasticity is not free and the loop question stays open. Note that SQ350 (parameter-matched loops, GPU approval pending) asks a different question and does not replace this test.
