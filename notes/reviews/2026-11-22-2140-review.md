## 2. Critique

**Framing and controls**
1. **The README headline uses the wrong control.** The bold result (E1 −3.6%, README line 122) and "3.6% lower loss than the standard transformer" (line 48) are both against E0. AGENTS §6 says that once E10 has run it is the control, and E10 shows E0 is 3.7% handicapped. The 21:10 review puts E1 vs E10 at about +0.14% (computed from its figures). C5 is PARTIAL, so the headline is an over-claim in the outsider-facing document.
2. **E10 is not implementation-matched beyond init.** Per `configs/e10_llama_ref.json`, E10 differs in init (flat 0.02 vs our residual scaling in `_init_weights`), RoPE layout (half-split vs the interleaved `Rotary`), and RMSNorm eps (1e-6). SQ234 tests only init. Phase 2b's E1-vs-E10 gap therefore mixes mixer, init and RoPE.
3. **Not param-matched.** E1 has 11.1M non-embedding params (README table) vs 10.62M for E0 and E10 (computed: +4.5%). AGENTS §5 and §6 require stating matched tokens/params and FLOPs. The "0.94x compute" figure is the analytic formula, which counts SWA as min(window, T/2). The kernel is a dense masked SDPA (`Attention._mask`), so the measured wall-clock is the real cost (point above).
4. **One scale.** Every result is one model size (13.8M total), one dataset and one token budget (200M is about 15 tokens/param). The stated goal of "scaling shapes" has no size axis, so the mixer result is not yet a scaling result.

**Statistics of the Phase 2b-5090 test**
5. **R2 tests reproduction, not the comparison.** Its ±0.004 tolerance is about five times the N1X E1-vs-E10 gap (0.0008), so a pass says nothing about the gap.
6. **R1 is not falsifiable as written.** "Sign matches the N1X's sign (whatever that turns out to be)" fixes no sign before scoring, and the expected gap is below the resolvable half-width (0.0018). It is close to a coin flip.
7. **The power calculation uses the wrong metric.** The wave-1 amendment makes the primary test the paired clean80 bpc difference, but the 0.0018 half-width assumes sd 0.0020 of contaminated val_loss. The clean80 sd is not measured in the files. Seed pairing is also nominal: E1 and E10 do not share per-step compute, so confirm the seed fixes the data order before calling the test paired.
8. **The primary population is reweighted by source.** SQ225: 52.3% of algorithmic-corpus val docs have >=80% overlap vs 22.9% of synthetic docs. Computed: clean80 keeps about 48% of algorithmic docs and 77% of synthetic docs. The per-config sensitivity table is marked "not_done". Wave 3a still reports one unstratified clean80 number.

**v2 design (`build_v2.py`, `notes/data-v2.md`)**
9. **Dedup recall at its own threshold is low.** Banding is 16x4 (manifest `lsh`). Candidate probability is 1-(1-s^4)^16, which gives about 0.64 at Jaccard 0.5, 0.89 at 0.6 and 0.99 at 0.7 (computed). Pairs near the threshold mostly escape merging, and connected components only partly compensate. Buckets above `max_bucket`=60 are "only chained" (`build_v2.py:190`), and their effect is unchecked. A test: sample pairs, compute true Jaccard, and measure recall at 0.5.
10. **The val set is selected for novelty and is longer.** Only 614 of 4,000 v1 val docs passed the <0.30 13-gram rule. The other 3,386 are train singletons chosen for low overlap. v2 val measures a different population, and its docs are longer: 348 tokens/doc vs 265 in v1 val (computed from the two manifests). Length is the axis C193 is about, so v2 val is confounded with the open question.
11. **Train is also shifted, and the eval set is contaminated differently.** 268,633 train docs were in duplicate families, and 18,871 were dropped by name-link to val (manifest). Train docs are longer too: 290 vs 266 tokens/doc (computed). clean80 is defined against v1 train (<0.8 overlap). v2 train removed the families of every v1 val candidate, so v2-trained models see less of clean80's near-duplicates than v1-trained ones. A v1-vs-v2 gap on clean80 mixes data quality with reduced contamination. Wave 3a's claim that the metric is "comparable" needs an overlap-binned check. The v2 train source mix is also unreported; dedup could shift it toward synthetic (spec).
12. **"200M tokens" means different things.** v2 train has 202.5M tokens, so a 200M run sees about 99% of it once; v1 sees about 76% (computed). A v2-vs-v1 comparison at matched tokens confounds dedup with data coverage. No dedup-only control exists.

**Method**
13. **LR "tuning" on one 20M seed.** SQ214 gives a 0.39% seed sd at 20M (E1 only). Choosing LRs from one seed per cell cannot separate LR differences below about 0.4% from noise (spec on the F0 decision; I did not read the full rule).
14. **The repetition mechanism is under-tested.** SQ228 terciles: E1 minus E0 is -1.84% in the lowest 8-gram-repeat tercile and +1.18% in the highest (computed from `sq_228.json` mean_y). Repeat share correlates 0.48 with log length, so the length story is not separated from it. Speculation: full attention copies repeated spans better (induction-like), so the deficit may be a recall effect at fixed length, not a length effect.

## 3. Alternative ideas

Throughput for estimates: SQ220 gives about 27 s per 20M-token E1 run on a 5090 (computed from 4.5 min per 200M). Other timings are speculation.

**I-a. Dedup-only control.** Hypothesis (spec): at 20M tokens, v2 and a random v1 subset give the same clean80 bpc within 0.4% (one 20M seed sd). Test: E1, 4 seeds each, on both sets, scored on clean80 and v2 val, with clean80 also binned by overlap with each model's own train (about 8 runs, ~4 min). Changes plans: no gain means the 23% token cost buys nothing, and A3 cells should stay on v1. Novelty: none in side-quests or the queue; SQ102 is the build, not an ablation.

**I-b. Source- and repetition-stratified scoring of existing checkpoints.** Hypothesis (spec): the E1-vs-E10 sign flips within at least one stratum (source x repeat tercile). Test: scoring only, on the 200M seed-0 E0, E1 and E10 checkpoints, using per-doc NLL (sq_225 and sq_228 inputs); CPU or a minute of GPU. Changes plans: a flip means the verdict depends on population choice, so strata must be registered before wave-1 results are read. Novelty: SQ225 defined the sensitivity table and left it "not_done"; SQ228 uses repeat share only as a covariate.

**I-c. Copy probe at fixed length.** Hypothesis (spec): E1's long-doc deficit is in-document recall, not length. Test: a synthetic copy/induction task with repeated random spans at 512 and 1,024 tokens, varying the distance, scored on E0, E1 and E10 (about 1 min). Changes plans: if the gap appears only on copied spans, the lever is attention placement (one extra full layer), not the mixer type. Novelty: SQ237 tests extrapolation and SQ219 removes context; neither is a copy probe.

**I-d. Param-matched E1.** Hypothesis (spec): the E1 advantage over E0 disappears at matched non-embedding params. Test: E1 with a narrower MLP to match 10.62M, 20M tokens, 4 seeds (about 2 min). Changes plans: if the advantage vanishes, C5's wording must say so. Novelty: not in the side-quest list; SQ234 covers init only.

**I-e. Ternary on the dense control.** Hypothesis (spec): the BitLinear penalty is the same for E10 as for E1, within 0.5%. Test: the E3 quantisation on E10's stack at 20M, 2 seeds, against unquantised E10 (about 3 min). Changes plans: an interaction would mean the FusionDance "fuse" claim depends on the mixer-ternary interaction, not on additive gains. Novelty: E3 is planned at 200M on the E1 stack only; the queue has no interaction test.

**I-f. Two-size check.** Hypothesis (spec): the E1-vs-E10 gap moves with model size. Test: d=512 variants of E1 and E10, 20M tokens, 3 seeds (speculated about 2 min per run). Changes plans: a gap that grows with size is the first point on the scaling axis the README promises; a shrinking gap weakens the linear-time pitch at our scale. Novelty: no size axis in side-quests or the queue.

**I-g. Wall-clock crossover at long context.** Hypothesis (spec): dense-masked SWA never beats full attention on wall-clock at our lengths, so the efficiency claim is testable only after a block-sparse kernel (SQ3). Test: forward+backward time for E1 vs E10 at 1k, 2k, 4k and 8k tokens, batch 1 (about 1 min). Changes plans: no crossover means the efficiency pitch is FLOP-analytic only and must be worded that way (AGENTS §0). Novelty: K1 registered memory and time; I did not read its result.
