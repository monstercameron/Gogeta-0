## 2. Critique

**2.1 The compute axis is a proxy for the goal, and ternary is scored where it cannot win (quoted, v0.5.27; computed).** Ternary arms are counted at full-precision FLOPs, so "matched compute" shows ternary as a pure cost (+9.5% clean80, C207). Its benefit is bytes. SQ378 measured packed size (16.0x) but no speed, and the goal-6 decision (D4, SQ371) is still a computed table. Goal 6 is batch-1 decode on a 24 GB card, and no assembled model has a measured decode speed (E8 is queued; v0.5.22 says the GPU speedup is not measured). So the headline is quality per training FLOP, and the deployment question is open.

**2.2 Loops are charged in FLOPs, not in decode reads (spec).** At batch 1, decode is usually bound by reading weights. A shared core is read r times per token, while an unshared stack of the same FLOPs reads each block once. Whether this shows up in wall time depends on caching, which we have not measured. SQ338 gives equal-wall-clock for GDN alone, and SQ403 shows A2s is sharply tuned to r = 2 (r = 1 is +27.9%). Loops therefore have a second cost axis that no current claim measures.

**2.3 The headline's comparator uses random r (code-read, quoted C212).** E27 trains with random r, and fixed r alone is worth 3.2% (C212). A2s trains with fixed r = 2. So part of the 16% may come from the training-depth choice, not from the assembly. A1a is the fixed-r E27-family arm (C212), but the README headline never reports A2s against it. I have not checked the A1a configs or whether its 200M horizon matches; the check is a re-read of existing numbers, so it needs no GPU.

**2.4 "Out of corpus" mixes two things (quoted SQ99, v0.5.38).** The 330 files are local stdlib and site-packages code. They differ from the OpenCoder corpus in style, not only in whether a document was seen. So C214 and C213 cannot separate (a) unseen-document memorisation from (b) a shift toward library-style code. The E61 decision rule rests on this set. SQ410 (dose-response) is the right test of (a), but nothing tests (b).

**2.5 Amendments are piling up inside running batches (quoted, v0.5.33-v0.5.35).** B1 gained amendment 1 (SQ350) while running, and amendment 3 rewrote SQ404's stated rationale after a descriptive scan. Each is disclosed and was registered before its result, and the Holm family is a good move. But the pattern (one batch, three amendments, reasons changed after data) is the forking-paths risk the AGENTS rules guard against. Suggest a frozen analysis script with a hash in PREDICTIONS.md before dispatch, and amendments only as new batches.

**2.6 Small-n claims carry headline wording (computed).** C209 (three seeds, one horizon), C214 (one out-of-corpus set) and C202 (three seeds per cell) are fine for their stated scope. But the README's status paragraph reads them as one story. Keep C214 scoped to the 330-file set in every sentence that cites it, since it is the sole basis for the E61 metric change.

## 3. Alternative ideas (speculation throughout)

**A. Measured decode frontier, not a computed one.** Hypothesis (spec): at batch 1 the loop's wall cost per token is well below its FLOP cost, so A2s's wall-time lead over E27 is close to its FLOP lead. Cheapest test: no training. Batch-1 per-token latency and tokens/s for A2s, A2f, E27, E24 and E20 at 200M (fp16, plus a packed-ternary path), about 15 GPU-min on the 5090. Result that changes plans: A2s more than 20% slower per token than E27 means D4 goes first and the headline gets a latency qualifier; equal or faster lowers the goal-6 risk. Novelty: SQ371 is computed and SQ198 used no trained model; a measured quality-vs-latency frontier is not queued.

**B. Schedule robustness of the assembly gain.** Hypothesis (spec): the gain survives a WSD (warmup-stable-decay) schedule, but the ternary cost moves with the late LR decay. Cheapest test: 20M tokens, E27, A2s and E24, cosine vs WSD, 2 seeds, about 10 GPU-min. Result that changes plans: a sign flip or a large shift in the gain means the 200M cosine-to-200M setting (SQ376 flags it) is a confound on C209. Novelty: SQ376 names the schedule confound but nothing tests it; SQ217 is EMA, not schedule.

**C. Domain transfer, not only corpus transfer.** Hypothesis (spec): the assembly gain depends on code syntax and vanishes on prose. Cheapest test: the in-repo version needs no download. Extract docstrings and comments from the existing corpus as a prose slice, train the three arms at 20M, 2 seeds, about 10 GPU-min. A true domain test needs a download, so it goes to the human under section 0a. Result that changes plans: if the gain vanishes on prose, C213's "memorisation" story is replaced by "code-specific", and the byte track's motivation changes. Novelty: D5 (SQ314) covers corpus transfer, not domain transfer.

**D. Fixed-r comparator for the headline (eval-only).** Hypothesis (spec): against a fixed-r E27 family, A2s keeps at least 10% of its 16% gap. Cheapest test: re-read A1a (fixed-r E27) against A2s on the same 200M clean80 scores, with no training, 0 GPU-min if the scores exist. Result that changes plans: a gap under 10% means C209's claim becomes "fixed r plus ternary and loops", and the README wording changes. Novelty: C212 and A1 exist, but no item reports the headline against the fixed-r comparator.

**E. Uncontaminated-training ablation on the out-of-corpus claim.** Hypothesis (spec): C214's loop advantage out of corpus comes from training-set overlap, not from the architecture. Cheapest test: train A2f and E24 at 20M on code-py-v2 documents with under 20% 13-gram overlap to the 330-file set, 2 seeds, about 10 GPU-min. Result that changes plans: if the loop advantage disappears on the filtered training set, C214 is a data artifact and the E61 metric change is premature. Novelty: partly SQ238 (dedup-only control) and SQ410 (dose-response). The cross-check against the out-of-corpus set is new.
