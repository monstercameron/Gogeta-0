# SQ395: compute-to-target from the existing curves (A2s vs E27)

**Status:** done (descriptive, not registered; zero GPU) · **Verdict:** the A2s advantage is not an endpoint effect. At every eval step from 1,000 to the end of training, A2s reaches E27's loss with 55-68% of E27's tokens (seed means; per seed 0.54-0.69); at the endpoint it needs 108.5, 118.2, 116.6 M tokens (seeds 2, 3, 4) to reach E27's final 200M val loss. Weighted by total training FLOPs (A2s costs 1.063x E27's) the ratios are 0.59-0.72 · **Links:** script `experiments/checks/sq395_compute_to_target.py`, results `experiments/checks/results/sq395.json`

## Method
Launcher logs of the A2s and E27 200M-token runs, seeds 2, 3, 4 (paired by seed, same data order; fleet pulls): `eval step N: val/loss` every 250 steps (128 val sequences of code-py-v2, nats/token, same eval set for both), 32,768 tokens per step, both on the same cosine schedule to 200M tokens. For each E27 eval step t >= 1,000: L = E27's val loss at t; t* = the first step at which A2s's piecewise-linear curve is at or below L; ratio(t) = t*/t. FLOP ratio from the FINAL lines of the logs (A2s 1.063x E27 on the seed mean; the registered amendment quotes 1.108x from the config accounting).

## Result (n = 3 seeds)
| | seed 2 | seed 3 | seed 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| final in-training val loss gap A2s vs E27 | -13.5% | -12.1% | -12.7% |
| tokens for A2s to reach E27's final loss | 108.5M | 118.2M | 116.6M |
| ratio at the endpoint | 0.542 | 0.591 | 0.583 |
Seed-mean ratio by step: 0.675 (step 1000), 0.631 (1500), 0.596 (2000), 0.563 (2750), 0.555 (3000, minimum), 0.575 (3500), 0.586 (4000), 0.611 (4500), 0.618 (5000), 0.602 (5750), 0.583 (6000), 0.572 (6103). Per-seed spread at a given step is at most 0.07. Multiply by 1.063 for the FLOP-matched version (0.59-0.72).

## Reading and caveats
- The headline gap does not come from the end of the schedule: the token ratio sits in a band of 0.55-0.68 from step 1,000 on, with a shallow minimum mid-training, i.e. A2s is "worth" roughly 1.5-1.8x tokens throughout. The horizon checks (SQ370/SQ380) do not need to move up on this evidence; they test the other direction (whether the gain persists at larger budgets), which these curves cannot.
- In-training val loss on 128 sequences, not clean80 bits/char: the final gap is -12 to -13.5% here vs -16.7% on clean80 (SQ379 devlog), so the in-training metric understates the clean80 gap. Same cosine schedule for both arms, so equal-step comparisons are equal-schedule comparisons; the ratios are not independent tuned-compute curves. n = 3 seeds; no CI (ratio is a deterministic transform of the curves).
