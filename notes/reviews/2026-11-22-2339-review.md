**2. LR transfer across width (muP or a 1/width rule) for E1.**

- Changes plans: a stable rule gives the G-1B LR from 14M, and E61 can be registered with a width-scaled LR. An unstable result means the final LR needs a direct 1B-class LR sweep, which costs money the plan does not yet budget.
- Novelty: no muP or LR-transfer entry in side-quests.md or the queue (keyword grep).

**3. EMA as a power tool, not only a bias fix (extends SQ217).**
- Hypothesis (spec): a weight EMA (decay 0.999) cuts the seed sd of final clean80 by at least 30%, so tie tests reach the 0.5% scale.
- Test: track an EMA shadow in 2 seeds each of E1 and E10 at d=256, 20M (about 10 min, no extra training). Compare the sd of raw and EMA losses.

- Novelty: SQ217 exists with a different purpose (LR robustness). The power angle is new.

**4. Unique-depth growth from a trained checkpoint.**
- Hypothesis (spec): appending one unshared GDN block to a trained E1 checkpoint and training 20M more tokens at a low LR recovers part of E32's advantage over E1 at lower total FLOPs than E32.
- Test: E1 checkpoint plus one appended block, 20M tokens at LR 1e-3, 2 seeds, against E32 and a from-scratch E1 at the same total FLOPs (about 15 min).
- Changes plans: if growth matches E32 at ≤60% of its FLOPs, growth is a cheaper route to the final model than training from scratch, and the G-1B plan should be revised. If it does not, C197 is closer to a width/capacity effect that only from-scratch training shows.
- Novelty: SQ272 (grow-in looping) grows the loop behind a gate. Growing unique blocks is not in the queue.

**5. Diagnose E10's d=256 seed spread from logs, not new runs.**

- Test: read the existing SQ243 training logs (CPU only, minutes). Count loss spikes and gradient-norm excursions per seed; compare the first 500 steps across seeds.

**6. Layer redundancy probe on E32 against E2.**
