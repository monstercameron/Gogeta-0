Experiment E11 · Reverse Kamehameha

Negative-eigenvalue Gated DeltaNet for language modelling

Charging config configs/e11_negeig.json

The question: Does letting GDN's write strength go negative (beta in (0, 2), i.e. negative eigenvalues) cost or help language modelling? E1's exact layout and recipe with one change (gdn_neg_eigval). Motivated by SQ6 arm (e): 100% parity in one pass and length generalisation, vs 97.9%/61.2% for looping (ledger C43).

What it will test, in plain English

A one-setting change to our "running notes" layer turned out to be amazing at keeping track of things. Does it also make the model better at writing code, or at least no worse?

Our Gated DeltaNet layers keep a small notebook of what they've read. Normally, each new word can only partly overwrite an old note. With "negative eigenvalues", a new word can also flip a note: write its opposite. That sounds odd, but it's exactly what you need for tasks like "is the number of 1s so far odd or even?", where each new 1 flips the answer.

In a small test (side quest SQ6), this one change scored 100% on that kind of task and kept working on sequences twice as long as it trained on. Our loop trick got 98%, but fell to 61% on the longer sequences.

E11 checks whether this change also helps, or at least doesn't hurt, the real job of predicting Python code. It's our best model (E1) with only this setting changed.

Prediction (written before the run)

Written 2026-11-22, before any run. Runs at E1's tuned LR (from K7), 3 seeds, same as E1's replication. Prediction: no language-modelling cost: mean full-val loss within +-1.0% of E1's mean, most likely within +-0.5%; training stable (no loss spikes > 0.5 after warmup; max grad-norm <= 1.5x E1's). Basis: the negative-eigenvalue paper (Grazzi et al., reported, not verified here) finds LM perplexity roughly unchanged while state tracking improves; our SQ6 shows the state-tracking gain at our scale. Criteria: 'free for LM' if |mean(E11) - mean(E1)| <= max(1%, 2x pooled seed std); 'helps LM' if mean(E11) < mean(E1) by more than 2x pooled std; 'hurts LM' if worse by more than 2x pooled std. If it is free or helps, the next step is neg-eig GDN inside the looped core (does it fix or replace what the loop was for?).

Why it matters

FusionDance's original bet was that looping would give the model the "keep track of state" ability that these fast layers lack. If a free one-setting change does that better, the loop may be needed for something else, or not at all. That would be a big, useful surprise.

Status

Registered, not run. Config configs/e11_negeig.json and the prediction are written. It runs after the learning-rate sweep (K7), with 3 seeds, next to E1.
