## 2. Critique

**2.1 FLOP accounting does not match the kernel (code-read, quoted).** model.py lines 4-8 say sliding-window attention runs as a dense boolean mask, so it is O(n^2) at our 1k context, and C12 is WRONG FOR OUR CODE. `flops_per_token` (lines 433-437) still credits SWA at its window. Every "matched FLOPs" comparison with SWA layers (E1 at 0.94x, E27 and A2s, E24 vs A2f) therefore counts work the kernel does not do. Wall-clock says the same: GDN alone is 1.62x dense wall time (README, SQ338), so equal FLOPs is not equal time. SQ244 and SQ310 (wall-clock) are still ideas, yet every per-FLOP headline leans on them.

**2.2 Unique parameters carry the headline, and the comparator is not matched on them (quoted, computed).** 1737 item 2.1 makes this point and I agree. A2s has about 1.6x the unique parameters of E27 (15.1 / 9.5, computed). The README still says "beat the full fusion by 16.7%" without that ratio. The parameter ratio belongs in C209's headline text, not only in its limitation.

**2.3 The loop verdict depends on which axis is read (quoted, spec).** At equal unique parameters, A2f 6x2 beats 6x1 by 1.33% with CI [-3.59, +0.99] (B1 G3, 2 seeds, 20M). That sits at the edge of its predicted range, and the CI spans zero. At matched FLOPs, unshared stacks buy about 2.4x more per FLOP (B1, C197). The README's headline metric is "quality per bit of weight memory and per FLOP" (DEVLOG line 17). Loops are the only arm that saves weight memory, so the bytes reading and the FLOPs reading may disagree, and the project reports only the FLOPs one. (spec) The goal-6 case for loops probably rests on bytes. No run compares loops with unshared stacks at equal bytes at a horizon past 20M.

**2.4 clean80 keeps documents with up to 79% 13-gram overlap (quoted).** The README defines clean80 as validation documents under 80% overlap. K9 found 41.7% of val docs at 80% or more (1,667 of 4,000). So clean80 removes only the most memorised documents. C213 says the assembly costs are "mostly in-distribution memorisation", but the primary metric still rewards memorisation in the 50-79% band. Clean50 is reported only in parts. This is a measurement gap, not a model result.

**2.5 C209's interval does not reproduce from its quoted per-seed values (computed).** Per-seed -17.05, -16.12, -16.82 (seeds 2-4) give mean -16.66, sample sd 0.48, se 0.28, and t(0.975, df 2) = 4.30. The script probably uses another scale or df. Check it before the README repeats the number.

A2 stacked the winners and tested them on fresh seeds 2-4, a fair test of the recipe. Recording the stack search would bound the selection effect.

## 3. Alternative ideas (all speculation)

**A. Loops on the bytes axis.** Hypothesis: loops pay on weight memory, not on FLOPs, so at equal packed bytes a looped core beats a larger unshared core. Cheapest test: CPU readout of B1 and SQ350 as a bytes-vs-clean80 frontier, then two extra 20M seeds of 6x1 vs 6x2 (about 20 GPU-min). Plan-changing result: if the equal-bytes gain stays under 2% with a CI spanning zero at 4 seeds, loops cannot be sold as weight compression, and the goal-6 pitch needs another reason. Novelty: SQ350 is equal params, SQ393 is ternary vs fp16, and SQ264 re-expresses per-bit for the factorial cells, not loops at equal bytes.

**B. Memorisation-band re-score of the headline pair (CPU).** Hypothesis: most of A2s's gap to E27 sits in documents with 50-80% overlap. Cheapest test: split the 2,333 clean80 docs into under-50% and 50-80% bands using the K9 overlap file, then read the paired A2s-E27 gap per band from the per-doc score files in experiments/stats/doclevel/ (not opened by me; 1737 names them). Minutes on CPU. Plan-changing result: if the gap sits in the 50-80% band, clean80 is the wrong primary metric and C209 moves to clean50 with the band reported. Novelty: SQ323 and SQ410 cover E1/E10 and the three costs, not the C209 pair.

**C. Equal-time replot of the headline (CPU, from logs).** Hypothesis: at equal measured wall-clock, A2s's advantage shrinks, because its dense SWA mask and about 10% extra compute cost time. Cheapest test: read wall_min from the A2 and E27 logs and replot clean80 against wall-clock, as SQ310 and SQ338 do for other arms. Plan-changing result: if A2s loses at equal time, the 16.7% cannot appear in goal-6 material. Novelty: SQ310 covers E0/E1/E2/E9/E10, and SQ338 covers the F2 cells, not A2s or E27.

**D. Long context for the assembled recipe (CPU).** Hypothesis: A2s keeps less of the linear-time long-context edge than cell 000 (SQ351: -60.7% vs dense beyond 1,024 tokens), because the shared core applies the same mixers r times. Cheapest test: SQ351's protocol (clean80 docs over 1,200 tokens, scored at 2,048) on A2s, A2f, E24 and E20, seeds 2-4, on CPU over a few hours. Plan-changing result: if A2s loses the edge, the copy fix (SQ383) comes before any goal-6 work. Novelty: SQ351 covers cells 000 and 100, SQ392 covers cell 000's RoPE control, and no loop cell has a long-context score.

**E. Batch-size robustness of the headline (20M, GPU).** Hypothesis: the E27-A2s gap depends on gradient noise, because the loop sums gradients over passes at one batch size. Cheapest test: E27 and A2s at batch 16 and 64, 2 seeds, 20M (8 runs, about 8 GPU-min each). Plan-changing result: a sign change at either batch makes the headline batch-conditional and needs a 200M check. Novelty: SQ404 varies the core LR, and SQ74 varies compile batch. Neither varies the training batch of the headline arms. Low priority.
