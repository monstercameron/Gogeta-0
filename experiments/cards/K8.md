# K8: External sanity reference in bits per character

## Question and prediction
- **Question:** are our ~0.23 bits/char on held-out Python plausible for this corpus, or a sign of a bug or train/val leakage (claim C94)?
- **Prediction (committed before running):** `experiments/checks/PREDICTIONS.md`, section "K8".
  - SmolLM2-135M at 0.28–0.45 bits/char; ours at 0.21–0.26.
  - Our doc-level bits/char matches the training-time value within 5%.
- **Pass criteria:**
  - K8a: ours ≥ 0.5 × SmolLM2's bits/char; otherwise check for leakage.
  - K8b: doc-level within 5% of `val_bits_per_char`.

- **When:** after Phase 2b. It's a GPU check: take the slot only when `bash experiments/launch/status.sh` shows it free with no GPU process.

## Results
**Early run (v0.4.28)** on our 4,000 val docs (3.94M chars), doc-level, `experiments/checks/results/K8.json`:
- **SmolLM2-135M: 0.4490 bits/char**; E0: 0.2209; E1: 0.2126.
- **K8a plausibility: FAIL.** Ours / SmolLM2 = 0.492 (E0) and 0.473 (E1), against a gate of ≥ 0.5. Our 14M models beat a 135M general model by just over 2×. That can be in-distribution training (SmolLM2 has never seen this synthetic annealing corpus), but the registered rule says to rule out train/val near-duplicate leakage before trusting any loss number. That's **K9**: a 13-gram overlap check, CPU, registered before it runs.
- **K8b arithmetic: FAIL.** Doc-level vs training-time bits/char differ by 6.0% (E0) and 6.2% (E1), against a ≤ 5% gate. This is the packed-window vs per-document protocol difference already measured and explained in C101 (−6.1%, reproduced to 0.014%). The registered gate didn't account for it. It's recorded as FAIL, not re-scored.
- **Prediction:** SmolLM2 in 0.28–0.45, **hit** (top edge). Ours in 0.21–0.26, **hit**. Ours better by 20–50%, **narrow miss** (51–53%). Within 5% of training-time, **miss** (see K8b).

## How to reuse this
- `score()` / `score_checkpoint()` give tokenizer-independent bits/char for any of our checkpoints (FusionDance or hf_llama). Use them to compare models with different tokenizers.
- Not a control: SmolLM2 differs in size, data and tokenizer.

## Caveats
- SmolLM2-135M is ~10× our size and trained on ~2T tokens of different data.
- Contamination with our corpus is possible and unchecked.
- It's a sanity ceiling, not a control.
