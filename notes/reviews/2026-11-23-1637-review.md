## 2. Critique

**2.1 SQ404's stated mechanism does not survive the optimizer (code-read, spec on size).** The registration premise (quoted from the v0.5.29 entry) is that shared weights "sum their gradients over passes at one global LR, so they step larger". The optimizer is `torch.optim.AdamW(..., fused=True)` (train.py:320). Adam divides by the running RMS of the gradient, so scaling a parameter's gradient by r leaves its update roughly unchanged (only eps differs). Summing over passes therefore does not enlarge the step by itself. The scale-dependent path is the global clip at train.py:352, `clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), grad_clip)`. Summed shared gradients inflate the total norm, so clipping can shrink the update of every parameter, not just the core. (spec) I would guess the clip is what makes shared arms behave differently, but this is unmeasured. Consequence: SQ404 tests the core's learning rate, and possibly the clip, not "step size" as written. Its registered "why" should be corrected by an amendment before the B1 readout is read. The prediction itself can stand.

**2.2 The SQ404 0.5 arm is an LR-matched core arm, not a step-size test (computed).** A2f runs at 6e-3 globally (A2 config). Multiplier 0.5 puts the core at 3e-3, which is E24's tuned LR (quoted, B1 SQ404 arm). Embeddings, head and inject stay at 6e-3. So the arm is closer to "core at E24's LR" than to a clean step-size control. Report it that way, and state which parameters moved.

**2.3 Two-seed gates are decided by point estimates (computed).** SQ402 and SQ404 use 2 seeds (df 1; the two-sided 95% t critical value is 12.7). The rule "closes more than half of the gap" (SQ404) and "within 0.5% of learned beta" (SQ402) are judged on point estimates whose intervals are wider than the thresholds. Amendment 2 already holds these at screening strength. Make the readout show the paired per-seed differences, so a reader can see the two numbers that decide the rule.

**2.4 C210's denominator needs one code-read (spec on risk).** The registered metric is "nats per target token incl. EOT" (DEVLOG v0.5.20). If the byte decoder's loss is summed over the same BPE tokens as the BPE head's loss (bytes within each token), the +14.4% is a model difference. If the byte side is normalised per byte or per patch, it is a units artifact. This is a read of the A4 loss code, not a run.

**2.5 C213's test removes overlap with one small sample (quoted SQ379, v0.5.26).** The out-of-corpus test is 330 files from 31 packages. The package-clustered CIs do not include seed variance (quoted). SQ400 adds seeds and leave-one-package-out (quoted, v0.5.32). Until it lands, C213 should keep its "hypothesis" label in the README, and the headline should not be read as "the costs are memorisation" (already so worded; check it stays that way).

## 3. Alternative ideas (speculation throughout; keyword-checked against side-quests.md and the queue)

**I-1. Is the shared-core loop cost a global-clip effect? (spec)**
- Hypothesis: at 200M, the global clip (train.py:352) fires more often on shared arms, because summed shared gradients raise the total norm, and that reduces their effective update. Part of D3's +3.2% (A2f vs E24) would then be an optimizer artifact.
- Cheapest test: CPU only, about 10 min. (a) A unit test that AdamW's update is invariant to a constant gradient scale (except eps). (b) From the existing 200M run logs for A2f, A3mx, E24 and E27, the fraction of steps with pre-clip norm above grad_clip and the mean pre-clip norm, per arm.
- What would change plans: clipping on more than about 10% of steps for the shared arms means the 200M loop-cost conclusion needs a clip-off or per-group-clip rerun at 20M (a GPU screen of about 10 min). Clipping on under 1% of steps means SQ404 is an LR test only, and its label changes.
- Novelty: SQ404 is the nearest item and it rests on the premise in 2.1. Keyword search for "adam" and "scale" finds no side quest on optimizer invariance or clipping. Not in the queue.

**I-2. Mean-over-passes versus sum-over-passes for shared gradients (spec).**
- Hypothesis: if I-1 is right, dividing the shared core's gradient by r (a mean over passes) changes little under Adam, but changes results where clipping was active. If the mean changes nothing, the sum-vs-step premise is dead and the loop cost is elsewhere.
- Cheapest test: 20M tokens, seeds 0-1, A2f with the core gradient summed (base) vs averaged, plus E24 as reference (4 runs, about 10 GPU-min). Run after B1 finishes, as a GPU screen.
- What would change plans: a difference above the replay floor (0.2-0.3%, DEVLOG v0.5.34) means clipping or epsilon matters, and I-1 is the next step. No difference means SQ404's premise is dropped, not the loop cost.
- Novelty: new. It is a cheap direct test of the premise in 2.1, which no queue item tests.

**I-3. Overlap dose-response for each assembly cost (C213's mechanism, spec).**
- Hypothesis: the ternary and loop costs grow with each document's share of train-overlapping 13-grams, while the assembly gain stays flat across overlap bins. That would show the costs are memorisation, the way C213 proposes.
- Cheapest test: inference only, CPU, about 15 min, on the existing 200M checkpoints of A2s/A2f, A3mx/E24 and E27/E20. Bin the clean-val docs into 4 to 5 overlap bands (K9 counts 13-gram overlap per doc; recompute if K9.json does not keep it). Report paired ratios per band.
- What would change plans: if the assembly gain also rises with overlap, C213's "gain survives out of corpus" needs a memorisation caveat in the headline. If the costs are flat across bands, C213 is weaker as a mechanism, and the costs are architectural.
- Novelty: the nearest items are SQ236 (E1 vs E10 membership gap, a binary held-in/unseen split) and SQ204 (5-gram floor). Neither gives a per-assembly-cost dose-response.

**I-4. Where in the document do the costs sit? (spec)**
- Hypothesis: the loop and ternary costs concentrate at absolute positions beyond the 256-token window (copy-at-distance, SQ347 and SQ383), not spread evenly. A fix would then be more full attention, not a different loop.
- Cheapest test: inference only, CPU, about 10 min, on clean80 for the same arms. Bin per-token loss by absolute position (0-127, 128-511, 512-1023) and compute paired ratios per bin.
- What would change plans: costs concentrated at 512+ make SQ383's fix the right next arm. Costs spread evenly mean the cost is per-token capacity, and SQ383 is not the lever.
- Novelty: SQ203 bins by distance to the last occurrence of a token, a different variable. SQ383 measures copy-at-distance on one gap, not the whole position profile. I found no absolute-position breakdown of the assembly costs.

**I-5. Does the 16.7% headline depend on document length? (spec)**
- Hypothesis: the assembly gain is larger on long documents (where state and attention roles separate) than on short ones, the way E1's advantage shrank past about 1,200 tokens (C193, PARTIAL).
- Cheapest test: inference only, CPU, about 10 min, on existing A2s and E27 clean80 per-doc outputs (or recompute with the saved checkpoints). Bin docs by token length and report paired ratios.
- What would change plans: a sign change across length bins means the headline needs a length qualifier before any "recipe" claim. A flat profile closes the question cheaply.
- Novelty: C193 covers E1 vs E0 only. No side quest tests the assembled arms' length profile (keyword search: "length" finds C193 and SQ99 only).
